# PLS Exercise Taxonomy

PLS uses exercise categories to ensure that learners do more than reproduce procedures.

## E1 — Recall

Recognize or reproduce terminology, notation, facts, or definitions.

Use sparingly. Recall is useful, but it is not sufficient evidence of understanding.

## E2 — Execute

Apply a demonstrated procedure or algorithm to a representative problem.

Examples: calculate, convert, simplify, compile, wire, run, measure.

## E3 — Interpret

Explain what a result, graph, program state, equation, measurement, or representation means.

## E4 — Modify

Change an example and predict or observe the effect.

Examples: alter a coefficient, instruction, circuit value, dataset, or program input.

## E5 — Diagnose

Find and explain an error, misconception, bug, failed assumption, or unexpected result.

## E6 — Derive / Justify

Show why a result or method follows from prior concepts. Depending on level, this may range from an informal argument to a formal proof.

## E7 — Design / Create

Construct a new solution, model, program, circuit, proof, experiment, or explanation from requirements.

## E8 — Explain-back

Explain a concept in the learner's own words, compare two ideas, or teach the concept to an imagined reader.

This category directly supports PLS-REQ-12.

## Coverage guidance

A substantial PLS resource SHOULD include multiple exercise categories.

For resources targeting PLS-3 or above, exercises SHOULD extend beyond E1 and E2 and include conceptual interpretation, justification, diagnosis, or creation.

Exercise difficulty and exercise category are separate dimensions. An E2 exercise may be difficult, while an E8 exercise may be simple.

## Recommended metadata

Exercises MAY be tagged in source material:

```text
PLS-EXERCISE: E5
LEVEL: 2
```

Multiple categories MAY be used when an exercise genuinely tests more than one mode of learning.
