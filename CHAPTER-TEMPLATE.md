# PLS Learning Unit Template

This template is recommended for chapters, lessons, modules, or other substantial learning units. It is not a mandatory visible heading structure.

## Metadata

```yaml
title: "..."
pls_entry: 0
pls_exit: 1
prerequisites:
  - "..."
learning_outcomes:
  - "..."
```

## 1. The question

Start with the problem, phenomenon, or question the learner is going to understand.

Avoid beginning with notation unless the notation itself is the subject.

## 2. Intuition

Build a first useful mental model in ordinary language.

State clearly when the model is simplified or incomplete.

## 3. Concrete example

Use a small example the learner can inspect completely.

Prefer examples that expose the underlying idea rather than merely demonstrate procedure.

## 4. Visual or alternate representation

When useful, represent the same idea in another way:

- diagram;
- graph;
- table;
- physical setup;
- code;
- simulation;
- timeline;
- state machine;
- geometric interpretation;
- other domain-appropriate representation.

## 5. Explore

Ask the learner to predict, change, test, compare, calculate, build, or observe something before giving the full formal treatment.

## 6. Language and notation

Introduce the real terminology and notation.

For each new item:

- name it;
- explain what it means;
- explain how to read it when useful;
- connect it to the intuitive model.

## 7. Formal definition

Give the precise definition appropriate to the target level.

If the formal definition corrects an earlier simplification, make that transition explicit.

## 8. Why it works

Provide the derivation, proof, mechanism, causal explanation, or design rationale appropriate to the discipline and target level.

Do not hide essential steps behind phrases such as "obviously" or "it is easy to see".

## 9. Worked example

Solve at least one representative problem completely.

Make the reasoning visible, not only the final operations.

## 10. Real application

Show why the concept matters in practice or how it connects to a broader topic.

## 11. Common mistakes

Include the misconceptions, traps, misleading intuitions, or implementation mistakes most likely to occur.

For each important mistake, explain why it is wrong.

## 12. Practice

Include a progression where appropriate:

1. recognition or recall;
2. guided application;
3. independent application;
4. variation or transfer;
5. challenge or synthesis.

## 13. Explain it back

Ask the learner to explain the central idea in their own words, justify a choice, interpret a result, or teach the concept to an imagined beginner.

## 14. Checkpoint

State what the learner should now be able to do.

Tie the checkpoint directly to the learning outcomes.

## 15. Next connection

End by showing how the concept connects to the next topic or a larger structure in the discipline.

---

## Author review questions

Before considering the unit complete, ask:

- Did we assume knowledge that was never stated?
- Did every new symbol and term get introduced?
- Is the first explanation intuitive enough for the declared entry level?
- Does the final treatment reach the declared exit level?
- Are any important reasoning steps missing?
- Did we simplify something without later stating its limitations?
- Does the learner actively use the idea?
- Can the learner distinguish conceptual understanding from memorized procedure?
